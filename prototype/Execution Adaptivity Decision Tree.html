Execution step · design note

Adapt to what the feed actually posts

The Execution step currently shows every control to every user — including controls that are meaningless for their feed. This note defines exactly what renders when, and why the combinatorics never explode.

P1

Two layers, never confused. The backfilled corpus (all parsed signals from this feed) decides what the step shows — once, per feed. Each individual signal decides what the engine does — at execution time. A feed is heterogeneous: 64% ranges and 36% single prices can coexist, so showing the ladder control is a corpus decision, not a signal decision.

P2

Three independent dimensions. Entry shape (none / single price / range) × target count (0 / 1 / many) × stop (present / absent) = 18 signal shapes. But the dimensions don't interact: entry handling never depends on how many targets exist. So the UI needs three small adapters, not eighteen branches.

P3

The skeleton stays put. 01 · Size → 02 · Entry → 03 · Exit always render, in that order. A section with nothing to configure collapses to a one-line statement of fact — it never disappears. The step stays learnable across feeds, and the preview ties keep working.

01Position size — no tree

Sizing depends on your account, never on signal shape. Always rendered, always identical.

02Entry — what renders

QDoes any backfilled signal post an entry range?
No No controls. One line: “This feed posts single entry prices — orders are placed at the posted price.”
Yes Render First touch / Midpoint / Ladder with the corpus stat: “64% of this feed’s signals post a range.”
Mixed feed Add: “Single-price signals fill at the posted price — this setting doesn’t affect them.”
“Enter now” signals exist Add: “Signals without a price fill at market.”

03Exit — what renders

QDoes any backfilled signal post a stop or a target?
No No source toggle — My own stop & target is the only mode (today’s “no risk management detected” case).
Yes Render the source toggle. When “Signal’s stops & targets” is selected, each sub-control has its own gate:
QDo all signals post both a stop and ≥ 1 target?
YesHide missing-handling entirely — there is nothing to fall back from.
NoRender Apply a fallback / Skip the signal with the stat: “8% of signals omit a stop or target.”
QDoes any signal post more than one target?
NoHide allocation and break-even toggle. One line: “Signals post a single target — the full position closes there.”
YesRender Equal split / Front-heavy / TP1 only with the stat.
Allocation = TP1 onlyHide the break-even toggle — TP1 closes 100%, there is no position left to protect.
Always Render Follow the author’s updates — authors post updates regardless of signal shape.

The engine matrix — what a config does to each signal

Because the dimensions are independent, eighteen shapes reduce to seven rules. Whatever combination a signal arrives in, the engine applies one rule per dimension:

Entry dimension
Range posted Entry mode applies exactly as configured — first touch, midpoint, or ladder across the range.
Single price posted One limit order at the posted price. Entry mode is bypassed, not misapplied.
No price (“enter now”) Market fill on verification.
Target dimension
Multiple targets Allocation applies as configured — equal, front-heavy, or TP1 only.
One target 100% closes there. Allocation is bypassed; break-even is moot.
No target Missing-handling decides: fallback take-profit (anchored on your average fill) or skip.
Stop dimension
No stop Missing-handling decides: fallback stop-loss (anchored on your average fill) or skip.

Worked combinations

The cases you asked about, traced through the matrix:

Signal arrives as Engine behavior
Range · 1 TP · SL Ladder fills across the range as configured. The single target takes 100% — your allocation setting is simply bypassed. Break-even is moot.
Single price · 3 TPs · SL One fill at the posted price — your entry mode is bypassed. Allocation splits across the three targets as configured; break-even applies after TP1.
Range · 3 TPs · no SL Ladder + allocation apply normally. The stop comes from your fallback (anchored on average fill) — or the signal is skipped, per your missing-handling choice.
“Enter now” · 0 TPs · no SL The degenerate case: market fill, then everything comes from fallbacks — or, more likely, you’ve set this feed to skip such signals.

Where the combinations should actually live: the preview

Mixed feeds mean no single sample signal can demonstrate the config. Instead of explaining combinatorics in copy, the plan preview should let you page through 2–3 representative signals from the backfill — e.g. Range · 3 TPs · Single · 1 TP · No stop — each rendered through your current config. The user sees that the ladder collapses to one fill, that one target takes 100%, that the fallback stop appears. One chip-switcher, zero paragraphs.

Open questions

  • Thresholds. If only 2% of signals post ranges, the control still governs those 2% — I'd show it whenever the count is non-zero, with the stat making rarity visible. Agree?
  • Hide vs. disable. The tree hides irrelevant controls (with a statement of fact in their place). Disabling instead would advertise capabilities the feed can't use — noise, in my view.
  • Corpus drift. If a feed starts posting ranges after configuration, the step should resurface the entry controls on next visit (defaulting to first-touch, the no-op-est option) — and possibly notify.
